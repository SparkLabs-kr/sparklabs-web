The founders walking into the SparkLabs office look different these days. A few years ago the first question was "how do I find a co-founder?" Now, more and more arrive alone, carrying an MVP they built themselves with AI. They code with Cursor, draft plans with Claude, and rough out customer emails with ChatGPT. With AI, one person can now do the work of ten.

I watch this change with genuine excitement. When we co-founded SparkLabs, the dream was always 'more impact with fewer resources.' That dream is now real for the solo founder. There are people like Matthew Gallager, who built roughly $2B in revenue in two years with AI alone, and cases like Maor Shlomo, whose solo-built service was acquired by Wix within six months. It is why we launched SparkClaw this year — a program for solo, AI-native founders.

But when I meet these builders now, there is one question I always ask.

> Thirty minutes demoing the MVP, twenty minutes explaining the tech. Then I ask "why does it have to be you?" — and surprisingly often, silence. In an era when AI writes the code, the scarcest resource is a person's WHY.

Watching more than 400 portfolio companies, I have seen one pattern repeat. What separates founders who survive from those who don't is not technology, funding, or team size. It is whether they have the strength to hold on through the crisis moment — the one you know is coming. That strength comes from mission. Honestly: I have almost never seen someone who started a company to make money survive to the end.

Remember mRNA vaccine technology? It was ignored by the market for decades, but there were people who believed in it and held on. When the external shock of COVID arrived, it was those who endured who saved the world. However powerful AI tools become, what carries you to the moment the market opens is, in the end, your WHY.

In the era of AI teammates, what a solo founder needs is not a new tech stack. One question, asked honestly in an empty room, is enough:

> "Why do I want to do this?" — only founders with a clear answer can wield AI as a real weapon.
