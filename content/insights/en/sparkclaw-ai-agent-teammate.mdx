Founders who reach out about SparkClaw tend to repeat the same sentence: "I make heavy use of AI tools." Cursor, Claude, Perplexity, n8n — honestly, everyone uses these now.

What I really want to know is the next question: "When funding lands or revenue starts and you can no longer handle everything alone or with a tiny team — which position will you hire first?"

Most pause. Then they answer: designer, marketer, developer. They are using AI tools, but they have never once considered which seat that AI could fill.

Here is the distinction I want to draw. Using AI as a tool and integrating AI into your process are completely different stories. Using a tool means reaching for it when needed — opening Claude to tidy meeting notes in Notion, opening another AI to generate an image. Useful. But that is a productivity gain, not a replaced teammate. Your cost structure doesn't change.

> The real shift happens when an AI agent owns the entire workflow of a specific role. When the structure means you never need to hire for that position, that is when the break-even point moves up.

Most startup cost is payroll. With an early team of five, 70–80% of monthly cash burn is people. When break-even comes slowly, it is usually not a revenue problem — costs are climbing too fast.

So these days I ask every founder: "In your current team design, is there a position an AI agent could take over?" I know it can be an uncomfortable question. But avoid it, and your runway runs out first.

## Roles where AI can be integrated into the process

Look at what is actually possible and the range is wider than you'd think:

- **First-line customer support** — 80% of inbound tickets are repetitive patterns. The agent triages and drafts; a human handles the remaining 20%.
- **Content drafting** — social posts, newsletters, blog posts. Teach it the brand voice, and the human becomes an editor.
- **Data collection and reporting** — the metrics report you used to pull by hand every week. Automate it with n8n + AI and you no longer need an ops hire.
- **QA and testing** — repetitive regression tests. The agent runs the scenarios; developers focus on core logic.

If you call this mere automation, you are still thinking at the tool level. Process integration means deciding at the organizational design stage: "this role belongs to AI going forward" — removing that seat from the hiring plan from day one. That difference buys you an extra two to three months of runway six months out.

> When I was building MapleStory at Nexon, my team and I had to make everything in the game ourselves. For today's solo founder, that team already exists — in the form of AI. The question is whether you design it in as a colleague, or leave it as a tool you sometimes open.

The founders SparkClaw is looking for are not the ones who use the most AI tools. They are the ones who have designed AI into the team structure. When applications open, plenty of people will write "I built my MVP alone with Cursor." Far fewer will write the next line: "So this position is currently owned by an agent, and I focus only on this part." The people who make that difference are the real solo founders of this era.

In the service you are building right now, is there even one position you could hand to an AI agent instead of hiring? Those are the founders I want to talk to.
